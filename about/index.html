About Toolbench

A small, independent set of calculators for jobs where a wrong number costs real money.

What this site is

Toolbench is a free collection of seven calculators: a concrete slab and bag estimator, a gravel, mulch and decking estimator, a marketplace fee and profit calculator for Etsy and TikTok Shop sellers, a trucking cost-per-mile and break-even calculator for owner-operators, a COBRA versus ACA health insurance comparison, an EV versus petrol fuel cost calculator, and a mouse sensitivity converter that translates eDPI and cm/360 between seven first-person shooters. Every tool runs in the browser, needs no account, and returns an answer while you type.

The site exists because most of the alternatives are harder to use than the arithmetic they perform. Concrete calculators hide their bag yields behind a sign-up wall. Marketplace fee calculators omit the costs that actually decide whether a product is worth selling. Sensitivity converters show an eDPI number as if it were portable between games, which it is not. These pages show the inputs, show the formulas, and show the assumptions — including the ones that could be wrong.

How the calculators are built

Each tool is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, served as static files from GitHub Pages. There is no server, no database, no framework and no third-party script. The calculation code for each tool lives in its own small file and is shared by the page and by an automated test suite, so the numbers you see come from the same functions the tests exercise.

The test suite covers unit conversions, rounding behaviour, edge cases such as zero or negative inputs, and worked examples whose answers were calculated by hand. When a formula is changed, the tests run again before the change is published.

Where the assumptions come from

  • Concrete. Bag yields (0.60, 0.45 and 0.30 cubic feet for 80, 60 and 40 lb bags) are the published figures for standard pre-mixed concrete. Volume and unit formulas are the standard US and metric conversions.
  • Marketplace fees. Default rates reflect Etsy’s and TikTok Shop’s published fee schedules for US sellers at the time of writing: Etsy’s 6.5 percent transaction fee plus 3 percent and $0.25 processing plus a 20 cent listing, and TikTok Shop’s 6 percent referral fee plus 2.9 percent and $0.30 processing. Every rate on that page can be edited, because fee schedules change and vary by country and category.
  • Materials. Bulk densities (1.35 ton/yd³ for pea gravel, 1.40 for crushed stone, 1.30 for river rock, 1.10 for topsoil, 0.40 for bark mulch) are typical loose delivered figures; the density field is editable because suppliers quote their own.
  • Trucking. Cost per mile is built from fuel, per-mile maintenance and insurance reserves, fixed costs and percentage fees on revenue. Reserves are the driver’s own planning figures, not an industry benchmark — the page says so and shows every line.
  • Health insurance. The comparison models premiums, the remaining deductible, a flat coinsurance share and the remaining out-of-pocket maximum. It is an educational estimate, not advice, and the page repeats that in the results panel.
  • EV running costs. One gallon of petrol is treated as 33.705 kWh, the US EPA figure, for the MPGe conversion. Charging losses are left to the efficiency figure you enter.
  • Sensitivity. Yaw values are the reference figures used by community converters (Valorant 0.07, CS2 and Apex 0.022, Overwatch 2 and Call of Duty 0.0066, Fortnite 0.005555, Rainbow Six Siege 0.00572958). The advanced panel lets you override them if a game update changes the scale.

What the site does not do

Toolbench does not have user accounts, does not accept uploads, does not store your inputs on a server, and does not use analytics or advertising cookies at present. Your theme preference is kept in your browser’s local storage and never leaves the device. If display advertising is added later, this page and the privacy policy will say so before any ad script runs, and the policy will explain how to opt out of personalised advertising.

Who runs it

Toolbench is an independent project maintained by a single developer, built in public in the GitHub repository that also hosts the site. Corrections are welcome: if a formula produces a number you can prove is wrong, or a fee rate has moved, the fastest route is an issue on that repository or a message through the contact page. Fixes are usually published the same week.

Editorial standards

Pages are written for the person doing the job, not for a search engine. Each calculator page includes an explanation of every input, the exact equations used, at least three worked examples with numbers you can check with a calculator, and answers to the questions that come up in practice. Where a figure is an estimate rather than a fact — a fee that varies by country, a bag yield that differs by brand — the page says so in the same sentence rather than in a footnote.

No page on this site recommends a product or service in exchange for payment, and there are no affiliate links.
